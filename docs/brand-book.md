## Our story

Every mother knows the cup of tea that goes cold. She makes it, the baby wakes, and by the time she gets back to it, it’s cold. Tomorrow it happens again.

In the first weeks after a baby arrives, people help. In many Pakistani families, relatives look after the new mother for forty days. In other places, people visit, bring food, or her partner takes time off work. Then everyone goes home. The advice keeps coming, but the help stops, and almost all of that advice is about the baby.

So the day starts to belong to the baby. Her own needs wait for "later": a meal, a shower, a quiet minute, a hot cup. Later doesn’t come.

Room for Mama was started by a mother of a baby in Lahore. She isn’t a doctor or a therapist. She lives the same kind of days as the mothers she talks to. She is just a little ahead of them: one nap ahead.

**What we believe:** a mother deserves room in her own day. Not a whole day off: a little room, every day.

**Why the name works:** it says who it’s for and what she gets. Say it out loud: the sounds are soft (r, m, m, m), so the name sounds calm, like what it offers.

**Still to add:** two or three lines in her own words about the moment she knew she needed room, and the first thing that helped. Only she can write these.

## Who it’s for

She is a mother of a baby or toddler. The family help has gone home, and now the baby’s naps and feeds decide her day.

| Where she lives | What her day looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Pakistan: Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad | A small household, or back at work. She still runs the home. |
| Abroad: the UK, the Gulf, North America | No grandmother nearby, often working, family in another time zone |
| Anywhere English is spoken | Partner back at work, no family or friends nearby to help |

**Her day:** she eats standing up, reads on her phone during night feeds, and plans nothing because the baby might wake.

**What she wants:**

- A routine that fits the naps, with room for a meal, a shower and some rest.
- A few minutes a day that are hers.
- To feel like herself again, and less guilty.
- Help that doesn’t judge her.

**What she doesn’t want:** another expert telling her what she’s doing wrong, anything that feels like a clinic or therapy, or one more thing to do.

Our guess, to check with the first ten mothers: she is about 24 to 38, speaks English and books on her phone.

## How we sound

We sound like a friend making you a cup of tea: warm, calm and plain. Nothing we write sounds like a clinic.

**Six rules:**

1. **Warm, not too sweet.** Kind words, said simply. No exclamation marks, no emoji.
2. **Calm.** Short sentences. One idea per line, one action per message.
3. **About her.** Talk about her day, not the baby’s schedule.
4. **No blame.** Never "should", "mistake" or anything that adds guilt.
5. **Homey, not clinical.** Words from a living room, not a waiting room.
6. **Honest about limits.** Say plainly what she can’t help with, and where to go instead.

**Who’s talking:** in everything a mother reads, the coach says "I", the mother is "you", and "we" only ever means the two of them together. Room for Mama is one woman, not a company. In this book, "we" means the people building the brand.

## How we look

Room for Mama looks like a storybook kitchen: one hand-drawn line, warm colour and soft round letters. Nothing clinical, nothing babyish.

&#91;image: Room for Mama brand board: the bun logo by day and at night, the five colours, the two fonts, a drawing, the icons and a voice line\]

**The logo:** the bun, a mother’s face with her eyes closed and her hair up in a top knot, on a peach halo. Beside it, "room *for* mama" is hand-lettered in soft round letters with a small bounce, with "for" in italics, as if said softly. The same "for" comes back in the tagline: A little room *for you.*

- Use it in Olive on Oat by day. At night the mark sits on an Oat disc and the words turn Oat.
- The stacked version is for square spaces. The favicon drops the face lines so it still reads at 16 pixels.
- Don’t retype the name, recolour the mark, or add a baby, heart or moon.

**The colours:** five colours from a warm kitchen.

| Colour | Code | What it’s for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oat | #F6EEE3 | The page background |
| Peach | #F3B08F | Warmth: the halo behind the logo and the offset print |
| Butter | #F5D06F | The lamp: the time she picks, the next call, the main button at night |
| Sage | #9BAF86 | Done: finished calls and confirmations |
| Olive | #34402A | Text and every drawn line |

At night the page turns deep olive (#1F2619) with Oat text, and Butter becomes the main button, like a lamp that’s on. Peach, Butter and Sage are never used for text. Every text colour is checked for easy reading, day and night.

**The fonts:** Fraunces, in its soft setting, for headlines: the same round storybook letters as the logo. Figtree for everything else: plain and easy to read at 3 a.m. Both are free on Google Fonts.

**Drawings and icons:** one hand-made brush line draws everything: the logo, five small scenes and 23 icons. Each drawing has one soft block of colour set slightly off its line, like a hand-pulled print. The scenes tell the story: the tea that goes cold, the tea that stays warm, her half hour, a chair kept for her, and the baby who wakes up.

**Our one bold idea, the offset print:** the moments that matter on screen (the time she picks, "You’re in" and the coach’s Needs you card) get a dark outline and a block of colour set just off it, like the drawings. Nothing else has a shadow except the Baby’s up sheet.

**Pictures:** the drawings lead. If photos are used, show real hands and warm cups, never a child’s face. Show the coach’s face only if she chooses. No stock photos of mothers, no hospital white, no yoga poses or lotus flowers.

## What we offer

One free first step, one main plan, and simple rules that never punish a mother because her baby woke up.

| Offer | What it is | In Pakistan | Abroad |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hello call | 20 minutes on video to meet, see if it fits, and leave with one small step | Free | Free |
| Make Room | Four half hours, one a week, at a time that suits you both | PKR 12,000 | US$120 |
| Top-up half hour | One extra call after Make Room | PKR 3,500 | US$35 |
| First ten mothers | Make Room at a lower founding price, for honest feedback. A review is welcome, not required. | PKR 8,000 | US$80 |

These prices are a starting point to test with the first ten mothers.

**The simple rules:**

- **Booking:** book any time from 6 hours to 6 weeks ahead.
- **Keep my spot:** the day before a hello call she taps Keep my spot. If she hasn’t tapped it 3 hours before and another mother is waiting, the time goes to her. If nobody is waiting, her call stays.
- **Baby’s up:** move any call for free, up to twice per call, until 10 minutes after it starts. The coach can do the same.
- **Paying:** Make Room is paid before the first call. Her times are kept for 2 days while she pays. Bank transfer, Raast or JazzCash in Pakistan; Wise abroad.
- **Changing her mind:** a full refund before the first call. After that, she can pause the rest for up to 8 weeks.
- **A missed call:** the first Make Room call she misses is put back, no questions asked.
- **Privacy:** the coach’s notes about her stay private. Emails, calendars and payment references never mention anything personal.

## Keeping mothers safe

Room for Mama is friendly support from a mother, not medical care. Being clear about that keeps mothers safe and keeps the brand calm.

| The coach does | The coach never does |
| --- | --- |
| Look at the mother’s real day and find time in it | Give sleep or feeding advice for the baby |
| Build one small change a week | Diagnose, name conditions or suggest medicine |
| Check in kindly | Act as a therapist or counsellor |
| Share what worked for her, as her own story | Promise results |

**The safety line,** on every booking screen, email and the website footer: "Room for Mama is friendly support, not medical care. If you feel low most days, please talk to your doctor or a helpline. If you have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, call a helpline or your local emergency number now."

**Anywhere else:** call the local emergency number, or find a free helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com).

**If a mother is having a very hard time:**

1. Listen, and thank her for telling you.
2. Share the helplines for her country; the app does it in one tap.
3. Encourage her to call her doctor or a helpline today. If she or the baby is in danger, ask her to call local emergency services now.
4. Pause the coaching if that helps, and check in gently, with her permission.
5. Keep a private note. Share it only if someone is in danger right now.

**The coach’s own limits:** three calls a day at most, only inside her own windows. The practice has to fit her life too.

## How it runs

The website does all the booking. Instagram and WhatsApp only send people to it.

| Job | How |
| --- | --- |
| Booking, moving calls, reminders, payments, notes | The Room for Mama booking app, built on Lovable |
| The website | roomformama.com, with booking built in |
| Instagram and WhatsApp | A link in the bio and one auto-reply that sends people to the website. No booking happens in messages |
| Video calls | Her own Google Meet link |
| Email | hello@roomformama.com, sent by the app |
| Payments | Bank transfer, Raast or JazzCash in Pakistan; Wise abroad |
| Calendar | The app adds every call to her phone’s calendar |
| Content | Instagram, three posts a week |

The app shows her only what needs her (a payment to check, a third move), and keeps an estimate of the minutes it saved her, called Time given back.

## The first eight weeks

Start with five mothers from her own circle, ask them for honest reviews, then grow on Instagram.

&#91;embedded content: the first eight weeks · 3 steps, 3 gates\]

Each step starts only when the goal before it (the "gate") is reached. Weeks count from launch day.

**What to post on Instagram:**

- **Real days:** sample routines built around naps.
- **Five-minute windows:** tiny things to do while the baby sleeps.
- **From one mother to another:** her own notes, only if she wants to share them.
- **Mothers’ words:** short reviews, only with written permission. Reviews from founding mothers say they paid a lower price.

**What to count each week:** messages in, hello calls booked, how many turned up, how many joined Make Room, calls moved, time given back, and mothers who sent a friend.
